# Four-day muscle-gain and strength programme

Updated: 14 November 2026. Approved replacement for new sessions. Existing saved sessions retain their own exercise snapshots, loads, alternatives, phase and prescriptions.

## Selection principles

Targeted isolation and supported pulling complement bench/squat strength anchors. There is no validated 90–95% targeting score. Comparative muscle-growth evidence, appropriate mechanics, loadability and repeatable execution guide the choices. Research supports principles; this exact combined programme has not been tested in a head-to-head trial.

The paused bench/squat, conventional deadlift, dips and standalone shoulder press leave the default plan. Incline pressing, barbell RDLs, supported pulling, direct side/rear-delt work, leg extensions and standing calf raises have explicit roles. The supported single-DB split squat has its own identity; historical aliases are never repointed.

## Reading the prescriptions

All listed sets are working sets; warm-ups are additional. Compounds target about 2 RIR; isolation work 1–2 RIR; abdominal work ends before momentum or loss of pelvic control. RIR means estimated additional clean repetitions, not a rep-max test. Use the printed exercise target; alternatives keep its sets, rest and phase-appropriate RIR.

For the first two exposures to a new movement, use two sets at about 3 RIR. Move toward the full prescription when form and recovery are consistent, through the existing manual phase setting. A phase change never rewrites a workout with logged sets.

## Evidence behind the selections

| Target            | Selection and evidence                                                                                                                                                                   | Primary source                                                           |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Hamstrings        | Seated curls beat prone curls for whole-hamstring growth in one 12-week trial. RDLs supply complementary hip-extension work.                                                             | [Maeo et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000002523)        |
| Triceps           | A 12-week trial favoured overhead cable extensions over neutral-arm extensions; repeat the loadable overhead movement twice weekly.                                                      | [Maeo et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/17461391.2022.2100279)       |
| Calves            | Standing raises favoured gastrocnemius and whole-calf growth, with similar soleus growth, in a small 12-week MRI trial. Your floor/DB setup is a practical translation.                  | [Kinoshita et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2023.1272106)     |
| Quadriceps        | Squats and extensions produced different regional responses. Keep both rather than treating squats as identical work for every quadriceps muscle.                                        | [Kassiano et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000005338)    |
| Side delts        | DB and cable raises produced similar growth in an eight-week trained-participant study. DBs are an efficient bilateral option in this gym.                                               | [Larsen et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2025.1611468)        |
| Elbow flexors     | Incline and preacher DB curls favoured different individual muscles, without a clear total elbow-flexor winner. Use incline curls and supported UNICA curls across the week.             | [Kobayashi et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2026.2741751)  |
| Chest             | One controlled trial found an upper-chest measurement-site advantage for incline pressing. Flat bench remains the strength anchor; incline angle and fly selection are coaching choices. | [Chaves et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7449336/) |
| Supported pulling | Select loadable pulldowns and supported rows for repeatable work. No trial proves the exact UNICA setup beats all other back exercises.                                                  | [Haugen et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-023-00713-4)        |
| Squat and hinge   | Use a controlled, comfortable squat depth and a repeatable RDL. Choosing RDL over conventional deadlift is goal-specific programming, not a demonstrated head-to-head growth victory.    | [Crawford et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000003701)    |

These are small studies, often in untrained participants, with particular machines and doses. Your UNICA curl and supported floor calf raise are practical translations, not exact reproductions. The incline-plus-preacher combination and exact UNICA row are coaching selections, not proven universal winners.

Glute sets share lower-body compounds, and arms receive additional pulling/pressing work. Front delts contribute to both upper-day presses; there is no extra shoulder-press slot in this block. Do not sum muscle-group counts into an effective-set total. Unilateral sets count once per muscle on each side, not twice. The 68-set target is a coaching starting dose, not an individual optimum.

## Progression and recovery review

1. **Familiarise first:** use two working sets at about 3 RIR for the first two exposures to new movements. Build toward the target tables when execution and recovery are consistent. Introduction remains a manual setting; no automatic phase increase is made.
2. **Select a working load by capacity:** it must fit both the printed range and reserve. For 5–8 reps at 2 RIR, six performed reps should leave roughly two clean reps available.
3. **Add clean repetitions first:** keep setup, range, rest and effort comparable. Increase resistance only after all prescribed working sets reach the printed upper bound at the target RIR.
4. **Complete added sets before adding weight:** when moving from two Introduction sets to three baseline sets, establish the full working-set count at the existing load first.
5. **Use the smallest available increment:** the app does not assume that the next barbell increase is 2.5 kg or that every stack jump is appropriate. Keep a heavier load only if the lower rep bound and target reserve remain achievable.
6. **No hidden extra-rep threshold:** lateral raises progress against 20 reps and overhead extensions against 15, as displayed. If the next equipment jump is too large, repeat the current load with consistent execution rather than forcing the increase.
7. **Log load conventions consistently:** barbell totals include the bar; DB values are per DB. The supported split squat uses one DB and a separate history ID from the older unsupported two-DB version. Machine, cable, Smith and free-weight loads are separate tracks.
8. **Bodyweight work:** improve clean reps and pelvic control first. A harder lever or version changes the comparison; use a consistent version when judging progress. One heel-walkout repetition is a full out-and-back cycle.
9. **Review every two weeks during an eight-week block:** check comparable performance, effort, setup and recovery. A three-exposure flag is a performance check, not proof of a physiological plateau or an automatic deload instruction.
10. **Count extra work:** demanding daily push-ups add pressing work; do not treat them as unlimited bonus volume.
